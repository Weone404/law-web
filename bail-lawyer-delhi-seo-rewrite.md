## Section: How Regular Bail Works in Delhi
*(New H2 — adds explanatory depth the live page lacks, and captures "how does bail work" / "regular bail process Delhi" search intent)*

Regular bail is sought **after** a person has been arrested and is in custody — as distinct from anticipatory bail, which is sought before arrest. In Delhi, a regular bail application is typically first moved before the Magistrate or Sessions Court holding jurisdiction over the police station where the FIR was registered. The court considers factors including the gravity of the offence, the accused's role, flight risk, likelihood of tampering with evidence or influencing witnesses, and cooperation with the investigation, before deciding whether to grant release — and on what conditions.

If bail is declined at the Magistrate or Sessions Court level, an application can be renewed or taken before the Delhi High Court, depending on the case.

## FAQs (Expanded)

**How fast can bail be filed?**
A bail application can often be prepared promptly once arrest, remand and FIR papers are available. The actual hearing date depends on the court's listing and the stage of the case.

**What is a surety bond?**
A surety bond is a formal undertaking — typically by a third party — supporting the accused's compliance with bail conditions. The court specifies the bond amount and the acceptable proof of solvency or identity.

**Can bail be filed the same day as arrest?**
It depends on the offence, the stage of arrest and remand, and court availability. Counsel can urgently assess whether an application may realistically be moved the same day.

**Is regular bail different from anticipatory bail?**
Yes. Regular bail is sought after arrest or custody, while anticipatory bail is pre-arrest protection sought when a person reasonably apprehends arrest, and follows a different procedure under Section 482 BNSS.

**What happens after bail is granted?**
The bond and surety documents must be verified and accepted by the court or jail authorities before release is processed. Bail conditions — such as appearing when required or not contacting witnesses — must then be followed throughout the case.

**Who can stand as a surety?**
Generally, a surety should be a person of verifiable identity and financial standing, often a relative or someone known to the accused, acceptable to the court based on the documents furnished.

**What if bail is rejected by the Sessions Court?**
If bail is declined, the application can, in appropriate cases, be renewed on fresh grounds or taken before the Delhi High Court for reconsideration.

**Does bail mean the case is over?**
No. Bail only addresses custody during the pendency of the case — the trial or investigation continues separately, and bail conditions must be maintained throughout.
