# Mutual Divorce Lawyer in Delhi

Where both spouses agree to end the marriage, mutual consent divorce is usually faster, less adversarial and less costly than a contested case. legalgroup's Delhi family law advocates help prepare the settlement and joint petition, and support clients through the court process until the decree is granted. Each spouse should obtain independent advice where their interests may differ.

## Legal Requirements Under Section 13B

A mutual consent divorce under Section 13B of the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955 (Section 28 under the Special Marriage Act, 1954 for civil marriages) requires:

- The spouses have lived separately for one year or more before filing
- Both agree that the marriage has broken down and they cannot live together
- Both agree, of their own free will, to end the marriage and to the terms — alimony, custody, and division of property

## The Two-Motion Process

1. **First Motion** — a joint petition is filed with both spouses' statements recorded before the court.
2. **Cooling-off period** — the second motion is ordinarily filed no earlier than six months after the first petition. It must generally be made within eighteen months of that petition, subject to the court's directions and any waiver application.
3. **Waiver of the cooling-off period** — the Supreme Court, in *Amardeep Singh v. Harveen Kaur* (2017), held that courts may waive this period where the marriage is truly irretrievable and all terms are settled; we assess whether a waiver application is realistic in your matter.
4. **Second Motion** — both spouses confirm their consent has not changed, and the court passes the decree of divorce.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How fast can a mutual divorce be finalised in Delhi?**
Where the waiver of the cooling-off period is granted, some matters conclude in a few months from filing. Without a waiver, the second motion is ordinarily heard after six months and should generally be made within eighteen months of the first petition.

**Can we change our mind after the first motion?**
Yes — either spouse can withdraw consent any time before the second motion is recorded, which is why the settlement terms need to be genuinely agreed, not signed under pressure.

**Do we need to have lived apart for exactly one year?**
The requirement is that the parties have lived separately for one year or more immediately before filing; separation doesn't require separate addresses, only that the marriage has practically ended.

**Is court appearance required for both spouses?**
Yes, for both motions, though courts have permitted video-conferencing appearance for a spouse living elsewhere in appropriate cases.
