# Maintenance Lawyer in Delhi

Maintenance cases decide whether a spouse, child or parent can meet basic living costs while a matrimonial dispute plays out — and often for years afterward. legalgroup's Delhi advocates represent both claimants seeking maintenance and respondents contesting the amount claimed.

## The Legal Framework

- **Section 144, Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023** (the successor to Section 125 of the CrPC) — a secular remedy available to a wife, children and parents unable to maintain themselves, against a person with sufficient means who neglects to support them
- **Section 24, Hindu Marriage Act, 1955** — maintenance pendente lite (interim maintenance) and litigation expenses during a pending divorce or judicial separation case
- **Hindu Adoption and Maintenance Act, 1956** — maintenance rights for Hindu wives, children and aged parents, independent of any matrimonial proceeding
- Comparable provisions under the Special Marriage Act and personal laws for other communities

## How the Amount Is Decided

Courts look at both parties' income and assets, the standard of living during the marriage, reasonable needs of the claimant and any children, and the paying spouse's actual capacity to pay. The Supreme Court's guidelines in *Rajnesh v. Neha* (2020) require both sides to file a detailed affidavit of assets, income and expenditure, which we prepare and scrutinise closely — this disclosure is often where cases are won or lost.

## What We Handle

- Filing maintenance applications under Section 144 BNSS before the Magistrate, or under Section 24 HMA in the family court where a matrimonial case is already pending
- Contesting inflated or unsubstantiated maintenance claims on behalf of respondents
- Enforcement of maintenance orders, including attachment of salary or property where payments are defaulted
- Modification applications where income or circumstances have materially changed on either side

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can a working wife claim maintenance?**
Yes, though courts weigh her own income against her reasonable needs and the husband's means; a wife with sufficient income of her own may receive a reduced amount or none, depending on the facts.

**Is there a maximum limit on maintenance?**
No statutory ceiling applies since the 2001 amendment to the maintenance provision; the amount is fixed case by case based on both parties' financial position.

**What happens if maintenance isn't paid?**
Non-payment can be enforced through the court, including attachment of the defaulter's salary or assets, and in some cases imprisonment for wilful default.

**Can maintenance be claimed before the divorce is finalised?**
Yes — interim maintenance (maintenance pendente lite) can be claimed and granted while a divorce or other matrimonial case is still pending.
