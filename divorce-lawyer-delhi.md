# Divorce Lawyer in Delhi

Filing for divorce is rarely just a legal step — it reshapes a household, finances and, where children are involved, their day-to-day life. legalgroup's Delhi-based family law advocates represent clients in contested divorce proceedings before Delhi's family courts, from the first petition through final decree.

## Grounds for Divorce We Handle

Delhi courts grant divorce under two principal statutes:

- **Hindu Marriage Act, 1955, Section 13** — cruelty, desertion for two years or more, adultery, conversion, mental disorder, communicable disease, and other statutory grounds
- **Special Marriage Act, 1954** — for marriages solemnised under civil or inter-faith law, with comparable and additional grounds

We also advise on annulment, judicial separation as an interim step, and restitution of conjugal rights proceedings where relevant.

## Where We File

Contested divorce petitions in Delhi are filed before the family court having jurisdiction over the matrimonial home or the parties' last shared residence — commonly Saket, Karkardooma, Rohini, Dwarka or Patiala House. We identify the correct forum before filing, which avoids delay from jurisdictional objections later.

## What a Contested Divorce Involves

1. **Petition and grounds** — drafting the petition with the specific statutory ground(s) and supporting facts.
2. **Interim applications** — maintenance pendente lite, custody arrangements and protection orders where needed, filed alongside or soon after the petition.
3. **Written statement and evidence** — pleadings close, followed by evidence and cross-examination.
4. **Mediation window** — courts frequently refer parties to mediation; we advise clients on when a settlement is genuinely preferable to continued litigation.
5. **Final arguments and decree** — argument on the evidence, followed by the court's decision.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How long does a contested divorce take in Delhi?**
Contested matters vary widely with the grounds argued and the parties' conduct; multi-year timelines are common where evidence and appeals are involved, which is one reason we assess early whether mutual consent is realistic.

**Can I file for divorce without my spouse's consent?**
Yes — a contested petition under Section 13 of the Hindu Marriage Act (or the equivalent under the Special Marriage Act) does not require the other spouse's agreement, provided a valid statutory ground is made out.

**What happens to maintenance and custody while the case is pending?**
Courts can and often do pass interim orders for maintenance and, where children are involved, temporary custody or visitation, while the main petition is heard.

**Is mutual consent divorce faster than contested divorce?**
Generally yes, where both spouses agree on terms. See our [Mutual Divorce Lawyer in Delhi](/services/family-law/mutual-divorce-lawyer-delhi) page for the process and timeline.
