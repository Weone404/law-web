## FAQs (Expanded)

**How do I choose a criminal lawyer in Delhi?**
Look for relevant criminal-law and court experience, clear communication, and a strategy grounded in the specific facts of your matter. A first consultation is the best way to assess fit.

**What courts do you appear in?**
Our panel includes counsel with experience before the Supreme Court, the Delhi High Court, and Delhi's district criminal courts. The relevant forum — Tis Hazari, Patiala House, Saket, Rohini, or Dwarka — depends on where your matter falls jurisdictionally.

**Do you handle BNS 2023 matters?**
Yes. We assess matters under India's current criminal laws — the BNS, BNSS and BSA — and explain how the applicable procedural provisions affect your specific case.

**How are criminal-law fees structured?**
Fees depend on the urgency, forum, complexity, and stage of the case. We explain the scope of work and professional fee structure clearly before engagement.

**Can I speak with a lawyer before receiving a notice?**
Yes. Early advice can help you preserve documents, respond carefully to any questioning, and evaluate protective remedies — including anticipatory bail — before the next step is taken against you.

**What is the difference between a criminal lawyer and a criminal advocate?**
In practice, the terms are often used interchangeably in India. "Advocate" is the formal designation for anyone enrolled with a State Bar Council and entitled to practise in court; "criminal lawyer" typically describes their area of practice.

**How long does a criminal trial take in Delhi?**
Timelines vary widely depending on the nature of the offence, the number of witnesses, court backlog, and whether the matter proceeds to full trial or is resolved earlier through bail, quashing, or compounding where available.

**Can a criminal case be transferred to a different Delhi court?**
Transfer applications are possible in limited circumstances — for instance, jurisdictional issues or concerns about a fair hearing — and are decided by the relevant Sessions Court or High Court based on the facts presented.
