## Section: What to Do When an FIR Is Registered Against You
*(New H2 — high-intent informational content the live page currently doesn't offer; strong candidate for "what to do if FIR is filed against me" searches)*

The steps taken in the first few days after an FIR shape everything that follows:

1. **Don't ignore a notice or summons** — non-appearance can itself create complications, including coercive steps by the investigating agency.
2. **Avoid giving an unplanned or unrepresented statement** — anything said during initial questioning can become part of the record.
3. **Preserve relevant evidence** — messages, documents, CCTV footage, or anything bearing on the allegations, before it becomes harder to access.
4. **Get an early assessment of bail and quashing options** — including whether anticipatory bail should be sought alongside, or instead of, a quashing petition.
5. **Keep a written record** of interactions with the investigating officer, including dates and what was discussed.

## Section: Understanding FIR Quashing Under Section 528 BNSS
*(New H2 — targets a specific, high-value legal search term the live page only defines briefly in one FAQ)*

Section 528 of the BNSS preserves the Delhi High Court's inherent powers to pass orders necessary to prevent abuse of the process of any court, or to secure the ends of justice — including quashing an FIR or criminal proceedings entirely. Courts typically consider quashing where:

- The allegations, even if taken at face value, do not disclose a criminal offence
- The dispute is essentially civil or commercial in nature, dressed up as a criminal complaint
- There is no legal evidence connecting the accused to the alleged offence
- Continuing the proceedings would amount to an abuse of the court's process

Quashing is discretionary and fact-specific — an early, honest assessment of whether your matter fits these categories is important before a petition is filed.

## FAQs (Expanded)

**Can an FIR be cancelled?**
An FIR is not simply cancelled on request. Depending on the facts, the Delhi High Court may consider quashing under Section 528 BNSS, or another remedy — such as anticipatory bail or a settlement in compoundable offences — may be more appropriate.

**What should I do if a false FIR is filed against me?**
Don't ignore notices or make an unplanned statement. Preserve relevant evidence and get advice on investigation, bail, and possible High Court relief before engaging further with the police.

**Is a lawyer allowed at the police station?**
A lawyer can advise you and assist with the legal process, though the exact role during questioning depends on the specific circumstances and applicable procedure.

**Can I file a counter-complaint?**
A counter-complaint may be possible where the facts disclose a separate offence. Counsel first reviews the evidence, jurisdiction, and the appropriate route for reporting it.

**What is Section 528 BNSS?**
Section 528 preserves the Delhi High Court's inherent powers, including the power to pass orders necessary to prevent abuse of process or secure the ends of justice — one basis on which an FIR can be quashed.

**How long does FIR quashing take in the Delhi High Court?**
Timelines vary based on the court's listing, the complexity of the matter, and whether interim relief is sought alongside the main petition. There's no fixed timeline, but urgency can be highlighted where warranted.

**Can an FIR be quashed if the complainant wants to settle?**
In certain compoundable and some non-compoundable offences of a personal or civil nature, courts have quashed proceedings where parties have genuinely settled, though this is assessed case by case and isn't available for all offence categories.

**What's the difference between an FIR and a complaint?**
An FIR (First Information Report) is registered by police for a cognisable offence and triggers a formal investigation. A complaint, by contrast, may be filed directly before a Magistrate for both cognisable and non-cognisable offences, following a different procedure.
